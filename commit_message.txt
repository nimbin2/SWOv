shared: the Wayland and drawing layer swas and swov will stand on

The first half of taking SDL out. swbr has never linked it — one surface on
the layer shell, a shm buffer pool, and everything drawn by hand into ARGB —
so the stack is proven; this lifts it out of swbr into three headers the other
two can use. Nothing is ported yet: swas and swov still link SDL3 after this
commit.

sw_draw.h is a Canvas: a pixel buffer and a clip rectangle. Rounded
rectangles and strokes come out of a signed distance field, text out of
stb_truetype with a per-size glyph cache, images out of a box filter, plus the
triangle, circle and annulus sector a radial menu is made of. It needs no
compositor and no GPU, which is the point as much as the short link line: a
whole frame can be drawn and then measured in a test.

sw_wl.h is a layer-shell window with that canvas in it — pointer, keyboard,
cursor-shape, two buffers, one event loop. The protocol marshalling is
swbr's hand-written glue, so there is still no wayland-scanner step.

sw_keymap.h reads the keymap the compositor sends over wl_keyboard.keymap and
answers what a key types. That is the piece that would otherwise be
libxkbcommon. It handles the four shift levels, AltGr, caps lock and the
keypad's NumLock; it does not handle dead keys, compose or layout groups,
which a search box does not need.

Checked, not assumed:

- Drawing, by rendering frames and counting pixels: a 20x20 rect covers 400
  and half that when clipped; r=10 corners lose their pixels and the centre
  stays solid; a stroke is a ring with an empty middle; a quarter annulus
  covers a quarter of the ring and r0 leaves the hole; a 2x2 image fills its
  box at the right colour, at half alpha, and a 64x64 checkerboard shrunk to
  8x8 averages to 127 rather than picking a pixel.
- The keymap, against libxkbcommon on the stock us, de, fr, es, gb, it, se,
  no, dk, pl, cz and ru layouts — every keycode at every modifier
  combination. The characters typed match exactly on all twelve. Two keysyms
  differ, PrintScreen and Pause, whose second level is selected by Control
  rather than Shift; neither types anything.
- The window, by running a demo on it under a headless sway and looking at
  the screenshot: rounded surface on the overlay layer, border, text at three
  sizes, shapes, all correct.

Three bugs the tests found rather than review: each key line in an xkb keymap
ends in "};", so scanning for that as the end of the symbols block stopped
after the first key; a key's symbol list is not the first bracket on the line,
because type[Group1] comes first; and uint16_t silently truncated the XF86
media keysyms.
